# Prose Pattern Audit

Produce clear, credible prose with deliberate rhetorical choices. Treat the patterns below as diagnostic signals, not banned constructions. Preserve an apparent violation when it earns its place through meaning, rhythm, emphasis, audience fit, or the writer's established voice.

Do not claim that the result will evade or pass AI-detection software. Optimize writing quality, specificity, authorship, and fidelity to the writer's intent.

## Workflow

1. Determine the audience, purpose, register, and writer's intended meaning.
2. Draft or revise for substance before performing the pattern audit.
3. Flag patterns only when they are conspicuous, repeated, empty, or unsupported.
4. For each flag, ask what the device accomplishes. Keep it if the answer is specific and worthwhile; otherwise revise or remove it.
5. Prefer the smallest change that fixes the problem. Do not sand away personality, useful irregularity, humor, cadence, or deliberate emphasis.
6. Read the finished prose for cumulative effects: repeated structures that work once may become mannerisms when stacked.
7. When revising user-written prose, preserve authorship. Do not introduce new facts, experiences, opinions, sources, or commitments.

## Pattern audit

### Mirrored negation

Watch for repeated formulas such as “not X, but Y,” “it isn't merely X; it is Y,” or “not X, not Y, just Z.”

- State the affirmative claim directly when the negation adds no substance.
- Keep the contrast when it corrects a real misconception or establishes a meaningful distinction.
- Prefer concrete and asymmetrical contrasts over two mirrored abstractions.

### Automatic grouping

Watch for reflexive lists of three, especially polished adjective triads or consecutive triads.

- Use the number of items the content requires: one, two, three, four, or more.
- Keep a triad when its completeness or rhythm is genuinely useful.
- Prefer specific items over interchangeable virtues such as “fast, reliable, and scalable.”

### Rhetorical question and answer

Watch for questions immediately answered by the writer, especially “Why does this matter?” and “The result?”

- State the point directly unless the question represents a question the reader is likely to be asking.
- Keep a question when it changes the reader's frame or creates useful participation.

### Inflated predicate

Watch for ornate substitutes for “is” and “has,” including “serves as,” “stands as,” “boasts,” and “represents” when they add no meaning.

- Prefer the plain verb.
- Keep the richer predicate when it describes an actual action or relationship.

### Participial tail

Watch for sentences ending in an appended phrase such as “highlighting,” “underscoring,” “demonstrating,” or “showcasing.”

- Delete it if it merely announces the importance of the preceding sentence.
- Promote it to a supported claim when it contributes new reasoning.

### False range

Watch for “from X to Y” constructions whose endpoints do not form a meaningful spectrum.

- Name the actual items or use a truthful category.
- Keep the range only when the endpoints establish real scope.

### Hedge stack

Watch for stacked qualifications such as “it is important to note that, arguably, while…”

- Remove throat-clearing and commit to the supported position.
- Retain qualifications that materially define uncertainty, evidence, scope, risk, or fairness.

### Vague authority

Watch for “experts argue,” “studies show,” “research suggests,” and similar unsupported attribution.

- Cite the source, identify the relevant body of evidence, own the judgment, or cut the claim.
- Never invent a source to make the sentence appear grounded.

### False suspense

Watch for drumrolls such as “here's the kicker,” “this is where it gets interesting,” and “but what happened next…”

- Deliver the content unless anticipation has a real narrative function.

### Analogy reflex

Watch for analogies added automatically to explain material already clearer in literal language.

- Keep an analogy only when it improves the reader's mental model or makes a difficult relationship easier to reason about.
- Prefer a direct metaphor or concrete description when it is stronger than a simile.
- Do not extend or repeat the analogy after it has done its work.

### Invented concept label

Watch for catchy labels or coined “effects,” “paradoxes,” and “creeps” presented as established concepts.

- Describe the phenomenon in plain language.
- Keep a coined term when the writer explicitly introduces it as their own useful shorthand and then defines it.

### Inspirational pivot

Watch for concrete material that abruptly expands into unsupported claims about humanity, the future, leadership, transformation, or meaning.

- End on the concrete consequence, decision, or next action.
- Keep the wider conclusion only when the preceding evidence earns it.

### Grandiosity

Watch for “pivotal moment,” “enduring legacy,” “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “redefine,” and similar inflated language.

- Scale the claim to the evidence. Mundane language can be more credible.
- Keep strong language when the facts and context support its actual magnitude.

### Repeated opener

Watch for successive sentences or clauses beginning with the same phrase.

- Vary the structure when repetition is accidental.
- Keep anaphora when it creates one deliberate rhetorical beat with a payoff.

### Metaphor flogging

Watch for one metaphor reused across a paragraph or document after its explanatory value is spent.

- Use it once and move on.
- Extend it only when the structure of the metaphor continues to reveal something precise.

## Whole-draft checks

- Replace abstractions with concrete nouns, actions, examples, consequences, or decisions where doing so improves meaning.
- Remove throat-clearing, generic transitions, and conclusions that merely restate the introduction.
- Vary sentence length and structure according to thought and cadence, not randomization.
- Prefer ordinary words when they are more exact than polished corporate language.
- Preserve domain terms that carry necessary technical meaning.
- Check that claims match the evidence and that citations support the precise claim made.
- Preserve deliberate fragments, repetition, parallelism, or irregularity when they sound intentional and accomplish something.

## Combining with voice skills

When another voice or brand skill applies:

1. Use that skill to establish vocabulary, cadence, register, humor, and characteristic reasoning.
2. Apply this audit without overriding those characteristics.
3. Resolve conflicts in favor of the writer's clear intent and established voice unless clarity, truthfulness, or audience comprehension would suffer.

Do not mistake a recognizable personal mannerism for a defect merely because it appears on the audit list.

## Output behavior

- When asked for a clean draft, return the revised prose without an unsolicited diagnostic report.
- When asked to review or explain changes, identify only material patterns and explain why each revision improves the piece.
- When a deliberate exception is worth preserving, leave it intact; mention the reasoning only if the user requested commentary.
- If the prose lacks enough personal knowledge, source material, or factual support to become genuinely specific, ask for that substance rather than manufacturing “human” texture.
